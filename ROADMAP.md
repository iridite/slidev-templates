# Roadmap

The roadmap prioritizes durable adoption and ecosystem usefulness over raw catalog size.

## Current foundation

- machine-readable registry with hosted and external ownership models;
- four distinct repository-hosted templates;
- structured template submission and contribution paths;
- registry, hosted-template contract, CLI, gallery, build, export, and health checks;
- explicit governance, provenance, security, and AI-assisted maintenance policies.

## Near term: make discovery genuinely useful

- publish the registry-powered gallery at a stable public URL;
- add richer compatibility and maintenance signals without manually copying volatile statistics;
- improve preview coverage for external entries;
- add category, audience, delivery, and workflow facets where they improve search;
- document a stable registry versioning and deprecation policy;
- collect feedback from authors of indexed projects and correct metadata quickly.

Success is measured by external use of the catalog, template submissions, downstream consumers, and successful scaffold/build reports—not only stars.

## Next: create downstream value

- provide a small installable CLI package that can search and scaffold hosted templates;
- expose a versioned registry endpoint suitable for tools and AI agents;
- add compatibility probes for current supported Node.js and Slidev versions;
- publish machine-readable health snapshots without rewriting historical registry metadata;
- support optional screenshots and richer gallery metadata generated from verified builds.

## Later: community stewardship

- invite additional maintainers when review volume or domain coverage justifies it;
- establish a lightweight proposal process for schema-breaking changes;
- add category reviewers for academic, developer, product, and organizational presentation workflows;
- coordinate with upstream authors rather than duplicating their release and support channels;
- document archival and removal procedures for abandoned, unsafe, or legally unclear entries.

## Non-goals

- collecting every public slide deck;
- competing with Slidev's theme registry by duplicating theme-only metadata;
- claiming ownership over external projects;
- vendoring branded or reciprocal-licensed projects merely to increase hosted-template count;
- using generated commits, fake issues, or artificial activity as adoption evidence.
